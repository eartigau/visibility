\section{Telluric star observations}
Telluric stars should be observed in the evening twilight. The purpose is to gather a library of featureless spectra with only telluric absorption imprint, so if you run into problems during a night, there's no problem not observing a telluric star on a given night. The general guidelines are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item We want to get both low and high airmass telluric star observations as these are equaly important for the library. You should check the day of the month for the beginning of the night; for an 'even' day (say the night begins on June 22$^{\rm nd}$), you observe the 'low' airmass target, and if it is an 'odd' day, you observe the 'high' airmass target.
    \item The table below lists the target to pick for all days through the year. Check the date and pick the low/high airmass target for that date.
    \item The target names are links, click on them to see the corresponding airmass plot for La Silla.
    \item Observe the target in both HA and HE modes. Even though it is seldom used, we are formally offering HA so we need to populate properly the telluric star library in that mode.
    \item Observe the hot star {\it either} in the evening or the morning twilight, you don't need to observe both.
    \item As long as the Sun is below the horizon, the sky is dark enough to observe a telluric star.
    \item Within the NIRPSteam P2, the targets are defined in the folder 60.A-9109(A) $\rightarrowtail$ COMM9 $\rightarrowtail$ Night\_obs $\rightarrowtail$ Telluric standards. You should copy-paste the observation blocks for HA and HE from there.

\end{itemize}

\section{What to do when things don't go as planned}

The purpose of telluric observations is to fill a library of (nearly) featureless spectra and obtain a representative observation in all absorption conditions. This is why we observe low and high airmass stars 50/50\% of the time. Here are general considerations for not-so-perfect nights :
\begin{itemize}
    \item {\bf If you miss the telluric on one night, that's fine}. We want globally to have, on average, about one per night, but if clouds will spoil the end of the night or if you are rushing to get a transit early in the night, just skip the telluric star.
    \item {\bf Do not get creative} with new targets! In the telluric correction process, we construct a template from all observations of a given star. If you add a random hot star not in the list below, it will end up being `orphaned' and most likely useless.
    \item The list of telluric stars below is designed to always have one target close to zenith and one close to the horizon at any sideral time. {\bf It is fine to observe any target in that list on a given night}. The dates are indicative only and should be used when things go as planned.
    \item If you need to observe with only one fibre, {\bf give priority to the HE fibre} but keep in mind that we require {\bf at least} one HA per week to improve the library of hot stars observed with that fibre.
\end{itemize}
